# Claims and disclosure

The rules that separate a comparison site from an advertorial. Everything here
is about one question a reader is entitled to ask: why should I believe this
ranking when you get paid when I click it?

## Sourcing a figure

Every number on the site belongs to one of four tiers, and the tier is visible
to the reader:

| Tier | Rendering | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vendor published | State plainly, with the check date | `$1.15/verification` · `Pricing verified: Jul 2026` |
| Third-party estimate | Label inline, never only in a footnote | `~$1.40/verification (third-party estimate)` |
| Aggregated user data | Name the platforms and date the pull | `Aggregated from G2 and Capterra, Jul 2026` |
| Editorial measurement | Name the rubric dimension and the date | `Editorial score 4.6/5 · rubric v3, Jul 2026` |

A figure that fits none of these tiers does not ship. Rewrite the sentence
without it. "Up to 70% less manual review" needs the study or the rubric
dimension behind it and the population it applies to; without that it is
marketing copy the site did not write and cannot defend.

Never publish a rounded or interpolated figure as though it were measured, and
never carry a competitor's marketing number into body copy without attribution.

## Distinguishing editorial score from user rating

These are two different claims and readers conflate them instantly when they are
rendered alike. Keep them apart: different visual treatment, different labels,
never summed or averaged together, and never presented so that the higher of the
two appears to be the site's verdict. When a provider's editorial score and user
rating diverge, that gap is useful information; show both and let the reader see
it.

## Disclosure

Three separate obligations, and satisfying one does not satisfy the others:

**Affiliate relationship.** The site earns commission on some outbound links.
Disclose above or inside the first block containing such a link, at body size
and body contrast. Repeat in the footer. Language stays plain: what the
relationship is and what it does not affect.

**Sponsored placement.** If a placement is paid, the label lives on that card,
adjacent to the provider name, not in a legend at the bottom of the page. It
must be legible in grayscale and readable to assistive technology, which rules
out a background tint as the only signal.

**Consistency between the two.** The methodology page, the disclosure line, and
any sales page describing placement options must describe the same arrangement
in the same terms. If placements are sold, no page anywhere may say placements
are never sold. This contradiction is the first thing a regulator, a competitor,
or a sceptical buyer will find, and it costs more than the placement earns.

## Ranking integrity

Rank order derives from the rubric. State the rubric's dimensions and their
weights on the methodology page, and keep the score on each card traceable to
those dimensions.

When a sponsored provider ranks well, say nothing extra: the label plus an
unchanged rank does the work. When it ranks poorly, the label plus the poor rank
is the strongest credibility signal the site can produce, so do not soften the
cons to compensate.

If a re-score changes a rank, the change is a content event: re-date the page,
and keep the methodology version visible so a returning reader can tell whether
the rubric moved or the provider did.

## Writing cons that carry weight

A con is useful when a buyer could decide against the provider on it. Test each
one: does it name a cost, a delay, a gap in coverage, or a dependency? "Pricing
is not published" and "onboarding can take several weeks" pass. "So feature-rich
it takes time to learn" fails, and one of those on a page discredits every other
con on it.

Keep the count symmetric with pros. An asymmetric card reads as a verdict
rendered before the evidence.

## Regulatory and factual care

The category itself is regulated, which raises the cost of a careless sentence:

- Never state or imply a certification the provider does not hold. Certifications
  are checkable and vendors do check.
- Standards get cited by identifier, and only where they genuinely apply.
- Ownership, funding, and jurisdiction facts go stale. Re-verify them on the
  same cycle as pricing.
- Nothing on the site constitutes compliance advice, and no page should read as
  though choosing a listed vendor discharges a legal obligation.

## Freshness

`Last verified` means a person re-checked the page's facts on that date. It is
not a build timestamp and not a copy-edit marker. Two rules follow: bump it only
after real re-verification, and re-verify before re-dating anything whose
pricing is older than two quarters. A visibly older date is a smaller problem
than a fresh date on a stale price, because only one of those is a false claim.
