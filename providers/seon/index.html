Quick verdict: SEON's edge is signal, not documents: it scores fraud risk from email, phone, IP, device, and browser data before a user ever submits an ID. That makes it a strong pre-KYC layer and AML complement, but it's not a document or biometric verification tool on its own — most teams pair it with a dedicated IDV platform.

What is SEON?

SEON is a fraud-prevention and AML platform founded in 2017 in Budapest by Tamás Kádár and Bence Jendruszák, now headquartered in Austin, Texas, with offices in Budapest, London, Singapore, and Jakarta. It serves more than 5,000 businesses, analyzing over 900 digital and device signals to score fraud risk — the company reports having helped prevent more than $300 billion in fraud losses.

Frequently asked questions

How much does SEON cost?

SEON's Starter plan begins at $699/month for entry-level fraud and AML screening; Premium pricing is custom and scales with usage and feature needs.

Does SEON verify identity documents?

No. SEON is a fraud-prevention and digital-footprint intelligence platform, typically used ahead of or alongside a document-verification provider rather than as a replacement for one.

What signals does SEON analyze?

SEON analyzes more than 900 digital and device signals, including email, phone, IP, device, and browser data, to score fraud risk in real time.

Does SEON do AML screening?

Yes. SEON combines fraud-risk scoring with AML screening in one workflow, in addition to its pre-KYC digital-footprint analysis.

What is the best alternative to SEON?

ComplyAdvantage is the best alternative if sanctions and PEP watchlist data depth matters more than pre-KYC device signals. Feedzai is the closest fit for enterprise-scale payment-fraud detection.

Who are SEON's main competitors?

SEON competes with ComplyAdvantage and Feedzai on the fraud/AML side, differentiating through its digital-footprint and device-intelligence signals.
