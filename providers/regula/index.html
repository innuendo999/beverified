Quick verdict: Regula is the deepest document-forensics option in identity verification, built for teams that need to catch sophisticated document fraud rather than run a full onboarding flow. Its 16,000-template library and on-device deployment suit banks and border agencies with strict data-residency rules, but it ships as an SDK and hardware reader, not a plug-and-play platform, and pricing is quoted per project rather than published.

What is Regula?

Regula is a document-authentication and biometric-verification technology company founded in 1992 and headquartered in Daugavpils, Latvia, under CEO Arif Mamedov and co-founder Ivan Shumsky. Rather than selling a hosted onboarding platform, Regula licenses a Document Reader SDK and Face SDK — plus proprietary hardware document readers — to banks, border-control agencies, and fintechs that need forensic-level document authentication built into their own systems.

Key features

  • •

    Document Reader SDK with OCR, MRZ parsing, and RFID/NFC chip reading for e-passports and ID cards.

  • •

    Forensic-level authenticity checks against a 16,000+ template library covering 254 countries and territories.

  • •

    Face SDK combining biometric face match with liveness detection.

  • •

    On-premise and on-device deployment options so verification data never leaves client infrastructure.

  • •

    Proprietary hardware document readers alongside the software SDKs.

  • •

    Support for 30+ languages across SDK and hardware interfaces.

Pricing

Regula does not publish pricing; it is quoted per project depending on whether you license the Document Reader/Face SDK, hardware readers, or both, and at what deployment scale. There is no published per-check or per-seat rate to benchmark against.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Regula cost?

Regula does not publish pricing. Its Document Reader SDK, Face SDK, and hardware document readers are quoted per project based on deployment scale, so there's no published per-check rate to compare against.

Does Regula offer a hosted, no-code platform?

No. Regula ships as an SDK and hardware toolkit for teams to integrate into their own systems, not a hosted, no-code onboarding platform.

Can Regula run on-premise?

Yes. On-premise and on-device deployment are core to Regula's positioning, letting verification data stay inside a client's own infrastructure — a requirement for many banks and government agencies.

How many document templates does Regula support?

Regula's library covers 16,000+ ID templates from 254 countries and territories, one of the largest in the category.

What is the best alternative to Regula?

Sumsub is the strongest alternative if you want a hosted, all-in-one platform with published pricing. Jumio is the closest match for enterprise-scale cloud onboarding.

Who are Regula's main competitors?

Regula competes with Jumio, Onfido, and Sumsub, though it differentiates by selling a document-authentication SDK and hardware readers rather than a full hosted onboarding platform.
