What Peru-Ready KYC/AML Actually Requires

Peru's financial regulator, the SBS (Superintendencia de Banca, Seguros y AFP), sets customer due diligence rules for banks and fintechs, while suspicious-activity reports route through the UIF, the country's financial intelligence unit. A provider built for this market reads Peruvian national ID documents (DNI) reliably, checks against local and international sanctions/PEP lists, and keeps latency low as onboarding volume spikes.

Core capabilities compliance teams typically require

  • DNI document parsing tuned to Peruvian ID formats
  • Sanctions/PEP screening covering both domestic and OFAC/UN lists
  • Transaction monitoring calibrated for SBS reporting thresholds
  • Support for crypto-exchange onboarding, where relevant, given Peru's growing digital-asset activity

How this differs from Brazil or Mexico

Unlike Brazil's CPF/CNPJ-centric checks or Mexico's CNBV framework, Peru-focused providers need to map directly to SBS supervisory categories and UIF reporting formats — a mismatch here is one of the most common reasons onboarding stacks fail local audits.

Regulatory context

Key references: SBS due-diligence resolutions, and UIF suspicious-activity reporting requirements.

Frequently asked questions

Who regulates KYC/AML in Peru?

The SBS (Superintendencia de Banca, Seguros y AFP) sets customer due diligence rules for banks and fintechs, while suspicious-activity reports route through the UIF, the country's financial intelligence unit.

What is a DNI in Peru?

The DNI (Documento Nacional de Identidad) is Peru's national identity document, and reliable parsing of its specific format is a baseline requirement for any KYC provider serving this market.

How is Peru different from Brazil or Mexico for KYC?

Unlike Brazil's CPF/CNPJ-centric checks or Mexico's CNBV framework, Peru-focused providers need to map directly to SBS supervisory categories and UIF reporting formats.

Does Peru have a growing crypto sector?

Peru has seen growing digital-asset activity, which has increased demand for KYC providers that can support crypto-exchange onboarding alongside traditional banking checks.

What sanctions lists do Peruvian KYC providers screen against?

Providers typically screen against both domestic watchlists and international lists such as OFAC and UN sanctions lists.

Why do onboarding stacks fail Peruvian audits?

A common failure point is using a generic LatAm KYC stack that doesn't map cleanly to SBS supervisory categories or UIF reporting formats, rather than one built specifically around them.
