What "India-Ready KYC" Really Means

The RBI issues the KYC Master Direction that regulated entities in India must follow, while FIU-IND, the financial intelligence unit, receives suspicious-transaction reports. A provider built for this market supports Aadhaar-enabled eKYC, cross-checks a customer's PAN, and integrates with CKYC, the Central KYC Registry that lets a verified record be reused across institutions.

The stacks you'll find here

  • Aadhaar-enabled eKYC integration
  • PAN cross-checks against tax and financial records
  • CKYC registry lookups to avoid re-verifying existing customers
  • UPI-speed decisioning to match real-time payment onboarding volume

How this differs from eKYC generally

Aadhaar-based eKYC is India's version of the broader eKYC category, but CKYC adds a shared national registry layer most other markets don't have.

Frequently asked questions

Who regulates KYC/AML in India?

The RBI (Reserve Bank of India) issues the KYC Master Direction that regulated entities must follow, while FIU-IND, the financial intelligence unit, receives suspicious-transaction reports.

What is Aadhaar-enabled eKYC?

Aadhaar-enabled eKYC lets a regulated entity verify identity electronically against India's Aadhaar biometric ID database, one of the largest national ID systems in the world.

What is CKYC?

The Central KYC Registry (CKYC) is a shared national database that stores verified KYC records, so a customer verified once by one institution doesn't need to repeat full KYC at another.

Why does UPI matter for KYC in India?

UPI's real-time payment volume creates onboarding pressure similar to Brazil's Pix — providers need fast decisioning to keep pace with instant account funding.

What is PAN and why is it used in KYC?

PAN (Permanent Account Number) is India's tax identification number, commonly cross-checked during KYC to link a verified identity to financial and tax records.

How does India's eKYC differ from eKYC elsewhere?

India's Aadhaar-based eKYC is unusually centralized compared to most markets, and the CKYC registry adds a shared-record layer that most other countries don't have.
