What Identity Verification Covers Beyond Onboarding

Identity verification is the umbrella discipline that includes eKYC's one-time onboarding check, but doesn't stop there. The same document-and-biometric engine gets reused at login, before a high-risk transaction, or after a password reset — anywhere a business needs proof that the person acting is still the person who signed up. That ongoing use is why presentation-attack detection and deepfake resistance matter more here than at onboarding alone.

Core capabilities compliance teams typically require

  • Document and biometric verification reused across the account lifecycle, not just at sign-up
  • Liveness and presentation-attack detection (PAD) tuned against injection attacks and deepfakes
  • Device and behavioral signals layered onto document/biometric checks for step-up authentication
  • Privacy-by-design data handling for biometric templates

Tip: teams that layer document fraud checks, biometrics, and device intelligence in a single API report up to 70% less manual review than teams running each check separately.

How this differs from eKYC

eKYC is the one-time gate at account opening. Identity verification is the broader, ongoing layer — the same vendor's engine often powers both, but a business buying only eKYC may still need a separate contract for post-onboarding fraud checks.

Regulatory context

Key references: ISO/IEC 30107, the presentation-attack-detection standard for biometric systems; NIST SP 800-63, the US digital identity guidelines; and GDPR/CCPA privacy-by-design obligations that govern how biometric templates are stored.

Frequently asked questions

Is identity verification the same as KYC?

KYC is typically the onboarding-moment check. Identity verification is the broader discipline that includes onboarding but also covers login, high-risk transactions, and account-recovery moments throughout the relationship.

How do vendors detect deepfakes?

Presentation attack detection (PAD) analyzes texture, depth, and motion cues that synthetic or replayed video typically fails to reproduce, per the ISO/IEC 30107 testing standard.

When does identity verification run besides onboarding?

Common triggers include login from a new device, a high-value or high-risk transaction, and password or account-recovery requests.

What is presentation attack detection?

Presentation attack detection is the set of techniques biometric systems use to tell a live human from a photo, mask, deepfake, or injected video feed, standardized under ISO/IEC 30107.

Do identity verification vendors store biometric data?

Practices vary by vendor and jurisdiction. Under privacy-by-design principles required by GDPR and CCPA, most vendors store a mathematical template rather than the raw image, with defined retention limits.

How is NIST SP 800-63 relevant to identity verification?

NIST SP 800-63 defines US federal assurance levels for identity proofing and authentication, and many commercial identity verification vendors benchmark their own controls against it.
