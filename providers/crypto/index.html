What Crypto KYC Verifies — On-Chain and Off-Chain

Crypto KYC does two jobs traditional KYC doesn't: it verifies the person behind a wallet, and it screens the wallet's on-chain history for exposure to sanctioned addresses, mixers, and darknet markets. The obligation to share that verification data between exchanges — not just check it internally — comes from FATF Recommendation 16, the Travel Rule.

Core capabilities compliance teams typically require

  • Wallet risk scoring against known illicit-activity clusters
  • Real-time sanctioned-address screening (OFAC SDN and equivalent lists)
  • Travel Rule message exchange between VASPs, typically over IVMS101-based protocols such as TRP, OpenVASP, Shyft, or VerifyVASP
  • Ongoing on-chain transaction monitoring (KYT) as a wallet's risk profile changes after onboarding

How this differs from AML

Crypto KYC screens wallets and on-chain flows specifically; general AML software screens traditional banking transactions and PEP/sanctions lists. The screening engines often overlap — several platforms in this list also appear in our AML category — but a crypto-specific vendor is built to read blockchain data natively.

Regulatory context

Key references: FATF Recommendation 16 (the Travel Rule), the EU's MiCA framework, and the VASP (Virtual Asset Service Provider) designation that determines which Travel Rule obligations apply to a given business.

Frequently asked questions

What is crypto KYC?

Crypto KYC verifies the identity of a person opening an exchange or wallet account, and pairs that with wallet-risk screening so the exchange knows whether the funds are tied to sanctioned or illicit addresses.

What's the difference between KYT and KYC?

KYC verifies who a person is at signup. Know Your Transaction (KYT) monitors on-chain activity afterward, since a wallet that looked clean at onboarding can later interact with a sanctioned address.

Do I need Travel Rule compliance software if I only serve retail crypto users?

Likely yes above the relevant transfer threshold. FATF Recommendation 16 applies to Virtual Asset Service Providers regardless of whether their end users are retail or institutional.

What is a VASP?

A Virtual Asset Service Provider (VASP) is any business that exchanges, transfers, or safekeeps virtual assets on behalf of customers — exchanges, custodians, and many wallet providers all qualify.

How does crypto KYC differ from traditional AML?

Crypto KYC screens wallets and on-chain transaction flows specifically. Traditional AML software screens banking transactions and PEP/sanctions lists; the two disciplines often share the same underlying screening engine.

Does MiCA require Travel Rule compliance?

MiCA governs crypto-asset issuance and service-provider licensing in the EU; the EU's separate Transfer of Funds Regulation (TFR) is what applies the Travel Rule to crypto transfers, and the two frameworks are designed to work together.
