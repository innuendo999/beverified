What Liveness Detection Actually Tests

Liveness detection is the narrow technology layer that proves a face in front of a camera is a live human, not a photo, mask, or injected video feed. It's one component inside a broader identity-verification workflow, not the workflow itself.

Core capabilities compliance teams typically require

  • Presentation-attack detection tuned to ISO/IEC 30107 test levels
  • Compatibility across phone camera sensors without specialized hardware
  • Sub-second capture with low false-rejection rates
  • Resistance to injection attacks, including virtual cameras and deepfake feeds

How this differs from Identity Verification

Liveness detection is the technology layer; identity verification is the full workflow that wraps liveness with document checks, watchlists, and step-up triggers across the account lifecycle.

Regulatory context

Key reference: ISO/IEC 30107, the presentation-attack-detection standard defining test levels for spoof resistance in biometric systems.

Frequently asked questions

What is liveness detection?

Liveness detection is the technology that proves a face in front of a camera belongs to a live human, not a photo, mask, deepfake, or injected video feed.

What's the difference between liveness detection and identity verification?

Liveness detection is one technology layer; identity verification is the full workflow that wraps liveness with document checks, watchlist screening, and step-up triggers across the account lifecycle.

What is presentation attack detection?

Presentation attack detection (PAD) is the formal testing standard, ISO/IEC 30107, that measures how well a liveness system resists photos, masks, and screen replays.

Does liveness detection require special hardware?

Leading providers work with a standard phone or laptop camera. Some offer higher-assurance 3D depth checks using structured light or additional sensors where the device supports it.

Can liveness detection be fooled by deepfakes?

Modern PAD engines are specifically tuned against injection attacks, including deepfake video feeds and virtual cameras, though detection quality varies meaningfully between vendors.

How fast is a typical liveness check?

Most consumer-facing liveness checks complete in one to three seconds, since long capture times increase both user drop-off and false rejections.
