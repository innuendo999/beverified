When we first published this guide in June 2025, most EU crypto firms were still running on national registrations and planning their MiCA applications. That window has closed. This version covers where the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation stands in October 2026: who it covers, what CASP authorisation requires, how the travel rule fits in, and how to check a firm's status.

## What MiCA covers

MiCA is [Regulation (EU) 2023/1114](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023R1114). As a regulation it applies directly in every Member State, without national transposition. It sets rules for two groups:

- **Issuers and offerors** of crypto-assets, with separate regimes for asset-referenced tokens (ARTs), e-money tokens (EMTs) and other crypto-assets, which need a white paper.
- **Crypto-asset service providers (CASPs)**, such as trading platforms, exchanges between crypto and funds or other crypto, custody, order execution and transmission, placing, transfer services, advice and portfolio management.

Some things are outside it. Article 2 excludes crypto-assets that already count as financial instruments, deposits or funds (unless they qualify as EMTs), and crypto-assets "that are unique and not fungible with other crypto-assets". The recitals add that a unique identifier alone does not make a token unique, and that services provided in a fully decentralised way, without any intermediary, are out of scope.

## Key dates and where things stand

| Date | What happened |
|---|---|
| 29 June 2023 | MiCA entered into force; some provisions applied from this date |
| 30 June 2024 | Titles III and IV (ARTs and EMTs) started to apply |
| 30 December 2024 | Everything else applied, including CASP authorisation; the Transfer of Funds Regulation applied too |
| End of Q1 2025 | ESMA's deadline for national authorities to bring CASPs into line on non-compliant ARTs and EMTs |
| 1 July 2026 | Latest end of the transitional period for CASPs operating under national law |

The application dates come from [Article 149](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-149-entry-force-and-application). Under [Article 143(3)](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-143-transitional-measures), firms that provided crypto-asset services under national law before 30 December 2024 could keep going until 1 July 2026, or until their MiCA application was granted or refused, whichever came first. Member States could shorten that period or not apply it at all. They chose different lengths, so the end date varied by country. [ESMA's December 2024 statement](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-12/ESMA75-453128700-1396_Statement_on_MiCA_transitional_measures.pdf) warned that a CASP authorised in one country could face a gap in another country with a shorter period.

## After the transitional period

[ESMA's April 2026 statement](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-04/ESMA75-113276571-1679_Statement_on_the_end_of_transitional_periods_under_MiCA.pdf) is blunt. From 1 July 2026, an entity serving EU clients without a MiCA licence breaches EU law and "must cease offering such services", whether or not MiCA has been fully implemented in a given Member State.

ESMA expected unauthorised firms to have a credible wind-down plan in place by that date. The plan had to give clients prior notice and avoid unnecessary harm, for example by moving their crypto-assets to an authorised CASP or a self-hosted wallet. Authorised CASPs taking over clients were told to run full AML/CFT onboarding, not just migrate accounts. National authorities are expected to take enforcement action against unauthorised services after 1 July 2026.

ESMA also reminded investors that MiCA protection attaches to the specific authorised EU legal entity. A sister company in the same group, or a non-EU affiliate, is not covered.

## CASP authorisation

### Who can apply

Under [Article 59](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/mica/mica-regulation/article-59/), a CASP needs a registered office in a Member State where it carries out at least part of its services, its place of effective management in the EU, and at least one director resident in the EU. Banks, investment firms, e-money institutions, UCITS management companies, AIFMs, CSDs and market operators can instead offer crypto services through the Article 60 notification route, without a separate CASP authorisation.

### Timelines

[Article 63](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/mica/mica-regulation/article-63/) gives the national authority 25 working days to check that an application is complete, then 40 working days from a complete application to grant or refuse. The authority can ask for more information up to the 20th working day of that period, which pauses the clock for up to 20 working days.

### Capital

[Article 67](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-67-prudential-requirements) requires the higher of a permanent minimum capital or one quarter of the previous year's fixed overheads. Our earlier version described this as capital "plus 25%"; it is the higher of the two, not the sum. The [minimum amounts](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/minimum-capital-requirements-crypto-asset) are:

| Class | Minimum capital | Services |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | EUR 50,000 | Order execution, placing, transfer services, reception and transmission of orders, advice, portfolio management |
| 2 | EUR 125,000 | Class 1 plus custody, exchange of crypto for funds, exchange of crypto for crypto |
| 3 | EUR 150,000 | Class 2 plus operating a trading platform |

The requirement can be met with Common Equity Tier 1 own funds, an insurance policy or comparable guarantee, or a mix.

### Passporting

Authorisation in one Member State lets a CASP serve clients in others. Under [Article 65](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/mica/mica-regulation/article-65/), it notifies its home authority of the countries, services and start date. The home authority passes this on within 10 working days, and the CASP can start once it hears back, or at the latest on the 15th calendar day after it notified.

### Reverse solicitation

[Article 61](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/mica/mica-regulation/article-61/) exempts a third-country firm only when an EU client starts the service "at its own exclusive initiative". If the firm solicits EU clients, the exemption is gone, and a contract clause or disclaimer cannot fix that. It also cannot use an unsolicited relationship to market new crypto-assets or services to that client. ESMA's April 2026 statement adds that outsourcing or delegation must not route EU client services through unauthorised third-country entities.

## Stablecoins

Titles III and IV set the rules for ART and EMT issuers. Two EMT rules matter for CASPs as well as issuers:

- [Article 50](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/mica/mica-regulation/article-50/) bans interest on EMTs, both from issuers and from CASPs providing services related to EMTs. Any benefit tied to how long a holder keeps the token counts as interest.
- [Article 54](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/mica/mica-regulation/article-54/) requires at least 30% of the funds received to be held in separate accounts at credit institutions, with the rest in low-risk, highly liquid instruments in the same currency.

In January 2025, [ESMA and the Commission published guidance](https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-and-european-commission-publish-guidance-non-mica-compliant-arts-and-emts) telling national authorities to make CASPs comply on non-MiCA-compliant ARTs and EMTs "as soon as possible, and no later than the end of Q1 2025."

## Travel rule and AML

Most AML obligations for CASPs come from outside MiCA: the AML directives and the recast [Transfer of Funds Regulation (EU) 2023/1113](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023R1113), which applies from 30 December 2024.

For every crypto transfer, the originator's CASP must send the originator's name, distributed ledger address or crypto-asset account number, and address with an official document number or customer number (or date and place of birth). It must also send the beneficiary's name and distributed ledger address or crypto-asset account number. The recitals say crypto transfers face the same requirements regardless of amount, and there is no exemption for low-value domestic transfers.

Self-hosted wallets get their own rule. Under Article 14(5), for a transfer of more than EUR 1,000 to a self-hosted address, the CASP must take adequate measures to assess whether the address is owned or controlled by its own customer.

The [EBA Travel Rule Guidelines](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/eba-issues-travel-rule-guidance-tackle-money-laundering-and-terrorist-financing-transfers-funds-and) have applied since 30 December 2024. They cover how to detect missing or incomplete information and what to do about it. For messaging networks and vendors, see our [travel rule provider ranking](/providers/travel-rule/).

On the AML side, the EU's new [Anti-Money Laundering Authority](https://www.amla.europa.eu/about-amla_en) in Frankfurt is due to select 40 obliged entities for direct supervision during 2027 and start supervising them in 2028.

Customer onboarding for a CASP is standard KYC with crypto-specific risk factors. ## Checking the ESMA register

The [ESMA Interim MiCA Register](https://www.esma.europa.eu/esmas-activities/digital-finance-and-innovation/markets-crypto-assets-regulation-mica) is a set of five CSV files: white papers for other crypto-assets, ART issuers, EMT issuers, authorised CASPs, and non-compliant entities. ESMA republishes it weekly from data the national authorities report, so a new authorisation can take time to appear. Withdrawn authorisations stay listed with the withdrawal date. ESMA planned to integrate the register into its IT systems around mid-2026.

White papers on the register have not been reviewed or approved by any national authority. A listing means the paper was notified, not that a regulator vouches for it.

## What comes next

On 4 December 2025 the Commission proposed a market integration and supervision package. ESMA's [press release](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-12/ESMA71-545613100-2843_ESMA_welcomes_Commission_s_ambitious_proposal_on_market_integration_-_press_release.pdf) describes it as moving "direct supervision of certain significant cross-border infrastructures and crypto-asset service providers" to ESMA. According to [Arendt's summary](https://www.arendt.com/mip-digital-finance/), most changes would apply 12 months after entry into force, and the proposal still needs agreement from the European Parliament and the Council. Until then, national authorities authorise and supervise CASPs.
