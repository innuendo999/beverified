iDenfy and ComplyCube are two of the few identity verification vendors that publish per-check prices and let you start without a sales call. Both check an ID document and a selfie, screen against sanctions and PEP lists and sell age checks. ComplyCube, from London, sells a developer-first API with low list prices, UK DVS certification and a choice of hosting region. iDenfy, from Kaunas in Lithuania, sells a platform with deeper KYB, billing only for approved checks and its own manual review team. Every figure below comes from the same records as our [ComplyCube review](/providers/complycube/) and [iDenfy review](/providers/idenfy/), checked on the date shown in each table.

## Pricing

ComplyCube prices each check on its own. On the Core plan, $299 a month that is credited against checks, a document check costs $0.75, photo liveness and face match $0.20, and standard AML screening $0.35. Starter costs $99 a month with higher per-check prices, and charges only successful verifications. iDenfy prices a full verification: $1.35 on Basic with a $135 monthly minimum, or $1.30 on Premium with a $325 minimum, with a 14-day trial that includes 10 free checks. For $0.50 more per check, iDenfy bills only approved verifications, so rejected checks cost nothing. For how both compare with the rest of the market, see the [KYC Pricing Index](/publications/kyc-pricing-index/) and our [budget KYC ranking](/providers/cheap-kyc/).

## What each platform covers

The widest gap is KYB. iDenfy checks 180+ company registries across 120 countries, maps full ownership structures, runs KYC on shareholders, retrieves company documents and monitors businesses after onboarding. ComplyCube returns shareholder and UBO data where available, but its company lookup is on the quoted Growth plan. ComplyCube lets you choose a hosting region; iDenfy keeps data in the EU by default and offers other jurisdictions on Enterprise. iDenfy's in-house team reviews verifications around the clock as a paid add-on; ComplyCube describes no review by its own staff.

## Certifications and lab tests

ComplyCube's identity service is on the UK register of digital verification services. iDenfy publishes its ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certificate; ComplyCube's ISO/IEC 27001 claim is vendor-stated. Both mention ISO/IEC 30107-3 liveness testing, but neither appears on iBeta's published list of confirmation letters, so both liveness claims count as vendor-stated in the score.

## What to test in a proof of concept

Public evidence cannot tell you how either vendor performs on your users. Run both on the same sample of real traffic from your main markets and ask each vendor for these numbers on that sample:

1. **Approval rate:** iDenfy's approved-only billing pays off only if a large share of your checks fail. Measure your approval rate first.
2. **False rejection rate:** genuine users who fail, and how many pass on a second try.
3. **Manual review:** how many sessions iDenfy's team reviews and how long a decision takes, and who reviews hard cases at ComplyCube.
4. **Liveness and injection attacks:** neither vendor has a lab letter we could confirm. Ask for test reports, or run your own attempts with masks, screens and a virtual camera.
5. **KYB depth in your markets:** if you onboard businesses, test both on companies from your main countries and compare registry hits and UBO results.
6. **Total cost at your volume:** for ComplyCube, the plan fee and each check you combine; for iDenfy, the per-verification fee, the minimum and the add-ons you need.

## Which should you choose?

Choose **ComplyCube** if you want lower list prices per check, need a UK DVS certified service for Right to Work or Right to Rent checks, want to pick a hosting region, or build on the web as well as mobile.

Choose **iDenfy** if you onboard businesses and need KYB with registry, UBO and company document checks, want to pay only for approved verifications, or want a vendor team to review hard cases around the clock.
