The US Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control published a [final rule on 26 August 2025](https://www.federalregister.gov/d/2025-16324) removing the Syrian Sanctions Regulations, 31 CFR Part 542, from the Code of Federal Regulations. The rule took effect the same day (90 FR 41505).

## What happened

Part 542 implemented the national emergency declared in Executive Order 13338 in 2004. That emergency ended on 30 June 2025, when the President signed [Executive Order 14312](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/06/providing-for-the-revocation-of-syria-sanctions/). From 1 July 2025 the order revoked E.O. 13338 and five other Syria orders (13399, 13460, 13572, 13573 and 13582). With nothing left to implement, OFAC deleted the regulations.

According to [OFAC FAQ 1220](https://ofac.treasury.gov/faqs/1220), persons blocked only under the revoked orders were removed from the SDN list as of 1 July 2025. At the same time OFAC designated 139 persons under E.O. 13894, which the new order kept and widened to cover former Assad regime officials and Captagon trafficking.

Three points from the rule mattered most for compliance teams:

- **This was a re-routing, not a clean slate.** Sanctions on Bashar al-Assad and other targets continued under E.O. 13894, counter-terrorism and counter-narcotics authorities.
- **Old conduct stays enforceable.** The Federal Register notice says the termination does not affect actions or penalties based on acts before 1 July 2025.
- **More rulemaking was coming.** OFAC said it would separately rename the Syria-Related Sanctions Regulations at 31 CFR Part 569.

## What it meant for screening teams

At the time we suggested five checks. They still describe the clean-up most firms did:

1. Confirm your screening provider loaded the July 2025 SDN removals and retired Part 542 programme tags, without dropping the E.O. 13894 and terrorism tags.
2. Update the sanctions risk policy and KYC narratives that referred to Syria as an embargoed country.
3. Expect fewer Syria-programme alerts, and sample newly cleared counterparties for hits under other programmes.
4. Before releasing blocked funds or trade flows, check for remaining terrorism, Captagon or accountability listings.
5. Keep historical case files: pre-July 2025 conduct can still be enforced.

## What has happened since

Status as of 18 October 2026, checked against OFAC, the Federal Register and the State Department.

- **25 September 2025:** OFAC renamed 31 CFR Part 569 the [Promoting Accountability for Assad and Regional Stabilization Sanctions Regulations](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-09-25/html/2025-18618.htm) (90 FR 46056). OFAC says a fuller set of these regulations will follow. PAARSS now appears as its own programme on OFAC's [programme list](https://ofac.treasury.gov/sanctions-programs-and-country-information).
- **2 September 2025:** the Commerce Department's BIS relaxed export controls, including a new License Exception Syria Peace and Prosperity, according to [Cleary Gottlieb](https://www.clearytradewatch.com/2026/07/u-s-government-removes-syria-from-list-of-state-sponsors-of-terrorism/).
- **November 2025:** Treasury, State and Commerce issued a Tri-Seal Advisory on sanctions and export control relief for Syria, later amended ([Willkie summary](https://complianceconcourse.willkie.com/articles/united-states-amends-tri-seal-advisory-to-reflect-caesar-act-repeal/)).
- **18 December 2025:** Congress repealed the Caesar Syria Civilian Protection Act of 2019, removing the threat of mandatory secondary sanctions on foreign persons dealing with the Syrian government.
- **24 August 2026:** after a 45-day notice period that began on 8 July 2026, Secretary of State Marco Rubio [rescinded Syria's designation as a State Sponsor of Terrorism](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesperson/2026/08/rescission-of-syrias-designation-as-a-state-sponsor-of-terrorism/). The same day State revoked the terrorist designation of Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham, OFAC [removed HTS from the SDN list](https://ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions/20260824) and revoked Syria General License 25, and an updated Tri-Seal Advisory was issued.

What has not changed, per [Baker McKenzie's summary](https://sanctionsnews.bakermckenzie.com/us-rescinds-syrias-state-sponsor-of-terrorism-designation-and-delists-hayat-tahrir-al-sham/): targeted SDN entries for the Assad network, Captagon traffickers, ISIS and al-Qaida affiliates and Iran-linked actors remain. Some Syria exports still need licences while Commerce finishes revising its rules. For screening, Syria is now a list-based risk, not a country block.
