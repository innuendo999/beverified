FinCEN wants to rewrite the part of the [CDD Rule](/glossary/fincen-cdd-rule/) that tells US banks how to handle the owners behind their business customers. The [proposal](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/136/CDD_NPRM_11_12_2026_508FINAL.pdf), released on 17 November 2026, turns a temporary relief from February into rule text, lists the data institutions must collect on each owner and closes a gap around taxpayer numbers. Teams that onboard legal entity customers in the US, and the vendors that run those checks for them, should read it before the comment window closes.

## What FinCEN announced

FinCEN [announced](https://www.fincen.gov/news/news-releases/fincen-issues-proposed-rule-strengthen-customer-due-diligence-requirements-covered-financial-institutions) a notice of proposed rulemaking to amend the customer due diligence requirements at [31 CFR 1010.230](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-B/section-1010.230). Treasury's [press release](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0691) presents it as part of its work under Executive Order 14406, "Restoring Integrity to America's Financial System", which the proposal dates to 19 May 2026.

The covered institutions are the ones the CDD Rule already applies to: banks, broker-dealers in securities, mutual funds, futures commission merchants and introducing brokers in commodities. The National Credit Union Administration [asked credit unions to comment](https://ncua.gov/newsroom/press-release/2026/fincen-proposes-rulemaking-strengthen-customer-due-diligence-requirements), since the Bank Secrecy Act covers every federally insured credit union.

## When owners have to be identified

Today's rule text asks institutions to identify and verify the [beneficial owners](/glossary/ultimate-beneficial-owner/) of a legal entity customer each time the customer opens a new account. A [FinCEN order](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2026-02/FinCEN-Order-CCDExceptiveRelief.pdf) of 13 February 2026 (FIN-2026-R001) already lets institutions skip that. The proposal would write the relief into the rule. Owners would be identified and verified:

1. when the legal entity customer first opens an account with the institution;
2. at any later time when the institution knows of facts that reasonably call the earlier beneficial ownership information into question;
3. as needed under the institution's risk-based procedures for ongoing customer due diligence.

FinCEN says this does not change the existing obligation to verify beneficial owners' identities, and that it is not meant to reduce ongoing due diligence. Verification still follows the procedures of the customer identification program.

## What to collect on each owner

The proposal lists the information to obtain on every beneficial owner: name, date of birth, a street address and an identification number. For a US person the number is a Social Security number, and the proposal says an Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN) is not an acceptable substitute. For a non-US person the options include a passport number with the country of issuance, an alien identification card number or the number of another government document with a photograph.

Three more changes matter for how the data is gathered:

- **Additional information.** The rule would say outright that institutions may collect more about beneficial owners where that helps manage risk or answer supervisors.
- **Third-party sources.** Institutions could take the required information from a third-party source instead of only from the person opening the account. The certification requirement would still apply.
- **Certification and confirmation.** At first account opening the person opening it still certifies that the information is accurate to the best of their knowledge, and the standard form stays optional. For later updates under ongoing due diligence, an institution could rely on the earlier certification plus an oral or written confirmation from the customer that the information is current, and it would have to keep a record of that confirmation.

The ownership threshold of 25 percent and the single control person stay as they are. FinCEN asks whether the list of example control positions, such as vice president or treasurer, should change.

## What it does not cover

The proposal is not the CDD Rule revision that the Corporate Transparency Act requires; FinCEN leaves that to a separate rulemaking. Since FinCEN's August 2026 rule, only foreign companies registered to do business in the US still report beneficial ownership to FinCEN, so US banks keep collecting it themselves under the CDD Rule.

## Dates and how to comment

Comments close 60 days after the proposal is published in the Federal Register; on 17 November 2026 the text had been released but not yet published, so there was no calendar deadline. The docket is FINCEN-2026-0298 and the regulation identifier is RIN 1506-AB78. Comments go through regulations.gov or by post to FinCEN's Regulatory and Strategic Affairs Division. A final rule would take effect 90 days after its own publication, and FinCEN asks whether that period should be longer or shorter.

## What it means for KYB checks

For most institutions the timing change is not new: the February relief has been available since it was issued. The parts that could change day-to-day work are the explicit list of data per owner, the end of ITINs as a stand-in for a Social Security number and the option to use third-party sources. If you rely on a [KYB provider](/providers/kyb/) to collect ownership data from registries, ask how it records the source of each data point, whether it captures confirmations from the customer with a timestamp, and how it flags owners identified only by an ITIN.
