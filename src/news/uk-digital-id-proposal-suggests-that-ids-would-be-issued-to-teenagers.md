When the UK government announced its digital ID scheme in September 2025, the headline was right to work checks. The detail that drew attention a few weeks later was the age. In its [2 October 2025 response](https://petition.parliament.uk/petitions/730194) to a petition against digital ID cards, the government said the credential would be "available for free to all UK citizens and legal residents aged 16 and over", and that it would consider through consultation "if this should be age 13 and over". We covered that on 14 October 2025.

## What was on offer

According to the government's [scheme explainer](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/digital-id-scheme-explainer/digital-id-scheme-explainer), the digital ID would hold a name, date of birth, nationality or residency status and a photo, and would be free to download. The petition response said: "By the end of this Parliament, employers will have to check the new digital ID when conducting a 'right to work' check." It also said obtaining one would not be compulsory, but it would be mandatory for some applications.

An age of 16 fits a credential built for work. Talk of 13 pointed somewhere else. A government credential held by young teenagers could be used for age checks online, which would bring it into the territory currently served by private age assurance providers such as [VerifyMy](/providers/verifymy/).

## Why it caused a row

The petition gathered 2,984,191 signatures and was debated in Parliament on 8 December 2025. The petition opposed digital ID cards in principle. Extending it to 13-year-olds raised harder questions: what data would be stored about children, who could ask to see it, and whether a credential designed for employment checks would turn into a general identity token.

For employers our point at the time was practical. Any new credential would sit alongside existing right to work routes, so HR teams would need to handle more than one type of evidence, plus edge cases like workers under 18 and people without a smartphone. That still holds, because the existing routes remain the only ones.

## What has happened since

Status as of 18 October 2026:

### January 2026: the mandatory element went

The government said in January 2026 that the digital ID would not be mandatory for British citizens, according to a [Lewis Silkin summary](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2026/03/23/uk-government-opens-consultation-on-digital-id-scheme-102mnlk) of the later consultation. [Full Fact](https://fullfact.org/government-tracker/digital-id/) reports that the government also said other digital IDs, such as biometric passports, would be accepted for right to work checks.

### March to May 2026: the consultation asked about age

The Cabinet Office consultation, [Making public services work for you with your digital identity](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/making-public-services-work-for-you-with-your-digital-identity), ran from 10 March to 5 May 2026, followed by a People's Panel that ended on 21 June. It stated there would be "no legal obligation for people to have or present the digital ID". On age, [Pinsent Masons reported](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/child-privacy-warning-uk-launches-consultation-digital-id) that the government's preference was 16, with a minimum age of 13 or no age limit as alternatives, and that a 13 threshold would let the ID be used for age verification on some sites restricted under the Online Safety Act. The consultation page says the government is analysing feedback; no formal response was published when we checked.

### July 2026: the scheme was dropped

On 18 July 2026, two days before he took office as Prime Minister, Reuters [reported](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/07/19/next-uk-prime-minister-andy-burnham-drops-digital-id-scheme) that Andy Burnham was dropping the national digital ID scheme and that its resources would "go instead to where it's most needed", and cited an Office for Budget Responsibility estimate that the scheme would have cost about £1.8 billion between 2026/27 and 2028/29. [Al Jazeera](https://aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/20/burnhams-move-to-scrap-uks-digital-id-plans-earns-cheers-and-criticism) reported that wider digital public services would continue. Full Fact, which had tracked the pledge after the Digital Access to Services Bill appeared in the 2026 King's Speech, now rates it "Not kept".

### What we could not confirm

The gov.uk explainer still carries its March 2026 text and has not been updated to say the scheme is cancelled. We found no gov.uk statement on what happens to the Digital Access to Services Bill. So the teenager question is closed for now: there is no plan to issue a state digital ID at 13 or 16.

### What it means for employers and platforms

Right to work checks continue under the Home Office [employer's guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide), updated for 1 October 2026, which has included guidance on digital identity verification through identity service providers since January 2022. Online age checks for teenagers remain with private providers under Ofcom's rules, which is the subject of our [TikTok age checks](/news/tiktoks-ai-age-checks/) story.
