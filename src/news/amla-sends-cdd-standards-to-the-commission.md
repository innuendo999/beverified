The EU's single AML rulebook now has its customer due diligence detail on paper. [AMLA](/glossary/amla/) has finished three of the technical standards that firms had been waiting for, and they are with the European Commission for adoption. For anyone buying an [eKYC](/providers/e-kyc/) or [identity verification](/providers/identity-verification/) service for EU customers, the CDD standard is close to a specification for the onboarding flow.

## What AMLA finalised

AMLA's [regulatory instruments page](https://www.amla.europa.eu/policy/regulatory-instruments_en) lists a published final report for each of the three drafts, all made under the [EU AML Regulation](/glossary/eu-aml-regulation/) (Regulation (EU) 2024/1624):

| Draft RTS | AMLR article | Consultation | What it covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer due diligence | 28(1) | 9 February to 8 May 2026 | Information to collect and verify for standard, simplified and enhanced due diligence, reliable sources, electronic identification |
| Business relationships, occasional and linked transactions | 19(9) | 9 February to 8 May 2026 | When a relationship exists, when CDD thresholds for occasional transactions apply, how to spot linked transactions |
| Group-wide arrangements | 16(4) and 17(3) | 16 April to 15 June 2026 | Minimum group-wide policies and information sharing, extra measures where third-country law blocks AMLR compliance |

The [CDD final report](https://www.amla.europa.eu/document/download/e15ab2e5-4074-4de8-bb9d-613dd8b7fdd1_en?filename=Final+Report+-+draft+RTS+28%281%29+AMLR.pdf) is dated 30 September 2026. AMLA's press release followed on 1 October, as [Regulation Tomorrow](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2026/10/amla-finalises-key-standards-for-the-private-sector/) reports. The CDD consultation drew 325 responses, and more than 1,600 stakeholders took part in the public hearing on 24 March 2026, the report says.

On 7 October the Luxembourg supervisor [CSSF](https://www.cssf.lu/en/2026/10/amla-adopts-key-regulatory-technical-standards/) wrote that the final reports had been sent to the Commission, which can still amend them before adoption. It told firms to finish a gap analysis of their policies, procedures and controls now.

## Remote verification: eID first, other methods with safeguards

To verify a natural person, Article 6(3) of the draft gives three routes: the identity document, passport or equivalent (or a verified copy), electronic identification means or qualified trust services, or the alternative solutions in Article 7. Article 6(4) adds that electronic identification can also be used face to face.

Recital 11 sets the order. Electronic identification at eIDAS assurance level "substantial" or "high", including European Digital Identity Wallets, and relevant qualified trust services should be used wherever possible.

Article 7 covers the rest: a person who cannot reasonably be expected to show the document in person and has no access to qualifying electronic identification. Here firms may use other remote solutions built on reliable, independent sources, with five safeguards:

- confirming that the person presenting the document is its holder;
- integrity and confidentiality of the communication;
- images, video, sound and data captured in a readable format and of sufficient quality;
- stopping the process on technical faults, a dropped connection or any doubt;
- valid, current data that is time-stamped and stored securely so it can be checked afterwards.

Under Article 7(3), the firm has to justify why the standard methods could not be used and show its supervisor that the alternative complies. Recital 11 says firms may rely on recognised technical standards to show this, and that each use is justified case by case.

## Data points, PEPs and sanctions

The [AMLA factsheet](https://www.amla.europa.eu/system/files/2026-09/Factsheet%20on%20RTS%20under%20Article%2028%281%29%20AMLR.pdf) spells out several data rules that affect how an onboarding form and a vendor's data extraction are set up:

- All given names and family names come from the identity document, passport, equivalent or electronic identification means.
- Place of birth includes at least the country and the city, town, village or equivalent locality.
- Country and city are mandatory parts of the address; other elements are collected where they exist.
- Firms take reasonable steps to find out whether a person has more than one nationality, but verifying one is enough.
- In low-risk cases a reduced set is verified: names, date and place of birth, and nationality or protection status, without the address. This is the simplified due diligence floor in Article 18.
- The central beneficial ownership register on its own is not enough to verify a beneficial owner.

For [politically exposed persons](/glossary/politically-exposed-person/), Article 17 requires a check of the customer, its beneficial owner and anyone a transaction is carried out for, before the relationship starts. Existing customers are rechecked at a risk-sensitive frequency and without delay when new information arrives or when the list of prominent public functions is updated. The tools can be automated, manual or both, as long as they are effective and proportionate. Sanctions screening covers customers and beneficial owners, and the factsheet names aliases, trade names and digital wallet addresses as relevant where available.

Existing customers have to be brought up to the new standard on a risk-sensitive basis: within one year for higher-risk customers and five years for all others, counted from the RTS's entry into force.

## The other two standards

The business relationships RTS is the structural layer. CDD always applies to a business relationship, while occasional transactions trigger CDD only above value thresholds, and the linked-transaction criteria stop customers from splitting payments to stay under them. At the consultation stage AMLA said it would not set extra lower thresholds for now, according to its [consultation page](https://www.amla.europa.eu/policy/public-consultations/consultation-draft-rts-criteria-identifying-business-relationships-occasional-and-linked_en). [A&O Shearman](https://finreg.aoshearman.com/amla-publishes-three-sets-of-final-draft-rts-under-eu-aml-package) notes that the final text was revised for clarity.

The group-wide RTS sets minimum requirements for governance, risk management, internal controls and secure information sharing across a group, plus extra measures where a third country's law stops a branch or subsidiary from applying the AMLR. For groups that run one KYC vendor across several EU entities, this is the text that governs how customer data moves between them.

## The central database hearing on 3 November

Separately, AMLA [held a public hearing](https://www.amla.europa.eu/events/public-hearing-draft-rts-establishing-central-amlcft-database-2026-11-03_en) on 3 November 2026, from 14:00 to 16:00 CET, on draft RTS for a central AML/CFT database under Article 11(6) of the AMLA Regulation. The database is meant to be a single entry point for standardised supervisory reporting and a store of supervisory information at entity and system level. The consultation opened on 7 October and closed on 3 November, [Regulation Tomorrow](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2026/10/amla-consults-on-draft-rts-for-aml-cft-central-database/) reports.

The reporting duty sits with supervisors. They would report on their own resources and activities, and on obliged entities' risk, sanctions, authorisations and fit and proper assessments. AMLA says the database should add no reporting costs for obliged entities, as [A&O Shearman](https://finreg.aoshearman.com/amla-consults-on-draft-rts-for-an-eu-wide-amlcft-database) summarises it. Even so, firms and trade associations from outside the financial sector used the hearing to question the data burden on small businesses, according to [AboutAML](https://aboutaml.substack.com/p/amla-wants-supervisors-to-share-more), which cites AML Intelligence. The same piece reads the draft as setting 15 July 2027 for the first submissions on some financial-sector information.

## What to ask an identity verification provider now

The Commission can still change the drafts. Even so, these are fair questions for any vendor whose contract will run past July 2027:

- Can the flow accept eIDAS electronic identification at substantial or high level, and EU Digital Identity Wallets, as the first route rather than an add-on?
- For document and selfie checks under Article 7, what evidence does it keep for each safeguard: holder match, capture quality, aborted sessions and time-stamped records?
- Can it log, per customer, why eID was not used, so you have the justification Article 7(3) asks for?
- Does data extraction return place of birth down to the city and support more than one nationality?
- Does PEP screening re-run automatically when public-function lists change, not only on a fixed cycle?
- How will it help remediate existing customers within the one-year and five-year windows?

## What happens next

The Commission now decides whether to adopt the three RTS as drafted or with changes. Each one enters into force 20 days after publication in the Official Journal and applies six months later, with some provisions for the obliged entities in Article 3(3)(n) and (o) AMLR applying from 10 July 2029. The AML Regulation applies from 10 July 2027 under its [Article 90](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/aml/amlr-regulation/article-90/) ([official text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A32024R1624)). The final central database RTS is still to come. We will update this article when the Commission adopts the standards.
