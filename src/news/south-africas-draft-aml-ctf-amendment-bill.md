On 13 December 2024 South Africa's National Treasury published the draft General Laws (Anti-Money Laundering and Combating Terrorism Financing) Amendment Bill, 2024, in Government Gazette No. 51772. The [Treasury media statement](https://www.treasury.gov.za/public%20comments/Draft%20General%20Laws/2024121901%20Media%20Statement%20-%20Publication%20of%202024%20General%20Laws%20Amendment%20Bill%20for%20Public%20Comment.pdf) gave the public until 6 February 2025 to comment. The bill was drafted with the Department of Trade, Industry and Competition, the Department of Social Development and the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC).

## Why Treasury wrote it

The bill targets what Treasury called the "remaining deficiencies identified by the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) mutual evaluation in 2021", plus issues raised while South Africa worked through its grey list action plan. Treasury also framed it as preparation for the next mutual evaluation, which it put at 2026/27. (Our January 2025 story said the next mutual evaluation report was expected in February 2025. Treasury's own statement gives 2026/27, and we have corrected it.)

## What the 2024 draft proposed

The bill amends four laws, each owned by a different minister:

| Law | Main proposals in the draft |
|---|---|
| Financial Intelligence Centre Act, 2001 | Fixes to targeted financial sanctions (sections 26A, 26B, 28A, 51A); updates on new technologies (section 42); customer due diligence for anonymous clients (section 46) |
| Companies Act, 2008 | Stronger remedial action and sanctions for failing beneficial ownership duties (sections 82 and 175) |
| Financial Sector Regulation Act, 2017 | Closing customer protection and market conduct gaps, tighter AML licensing and regulation, more licensing and enforcement powers |
| Nonprofit Organisations Act, 1997 | Maximum fines and prison terms for offences (section 30) |

There are also technical amendments to other anti-money laundering and anti-corruption laws.

## What it means for businesses

Most of the changes tighten rules that already exist rather than invent new ones. Companies face firmer consequences for not keeping beneficial ownership information, which matters to anyone doing [KYB checks on South African entities](/providers/kyb/). The FIC Act changes on new technologies and anonymous clients feed straight into how accountable institutions onboard customers and run [AML screening and monitoring](/providers/aml/).

Local vendors pitch to that demand. [RelyComply](/providers/relycomply/), headquartered in Cape Town, is one example of a South African platform that combines KYC, KYB and AML in one product. For tools that cover more countries, see our [regions hub](/regions/).

The bill does not change any duty by itself until Parliament passes it. Firms still work under the FIC Act as it stands today.

## What has happened since

**South Africa left the grey list.** At its October 2025 plenary the FATF published an updated list of [jurisdictions under increased monitoring](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/High-risk-and-other-monitored-jurisdictions/increased-monitoring-october-2025.html) dated 24 October 2025. South Africa, Nigeria, Mozambique and Burkina Faso were listed as no longer subject to increased monitoring.

**The EU followed.** [Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/83](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2026/83/oj/eng), adopted on 4 December 2025, removed South Africa from the EU list of high-risk third countries with effect from 29 January 2026. EU obliged entities no longer have to apply the mandatory high-risk country measures to South African customers on that basis alone. Our story on [Kenya and Côte d'Ivoire joining the EU list](/news/kenya-and-cote-divoire-added-to-european-commissions-aml-high-risk-third-country-list/) covers how that list moves.

**A revised bill went out for comment.** On 15 January 2026 Treasury announced the [General Laws (AML/CTF) Amendment Bill, 2025](https://www.treasury.gov.za/comm_media/press/2026/2026011501%20Media%20Statement%20-%20General%20Laws%20Amendment%20Bill.pdf), published in Government Gazette No. 53955 on 14 January 2026, with comments due by 13 February 2026. It keeps the 2024 structure and adds a few items: the FIC could share information with the Public Procurement Office and the Border Management Authority, and share information obtained through lifestyle audits, and section 41A would extend the FIC Act provisions covered by the Protection of Personal Information Act, 2013.

**It is not law yet.** As of October 2026 the [Parliamentary Monitoring Group](https://pmg.org.za/bill/1318/) lists the bill as a draft (X-2025) with no committee or House stages recorded.

**The next test is under way.** Treasury says the next FATF mutual evaluation of South Africa is set to start in mid-2026 and conclude in October 2027.
