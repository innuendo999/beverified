On 25 September 2025 the European Commission opened infringement procedures against 11 member states over access to beneficial ownership registers. Belgium, Croatia, Cyprus, Denmark, Estonia, Germany, Greece, Italy, Poland, Slovakia and Sweden had not fully notified national measures transposing the Sixth Anti-Money Laundering Directive (AMLD6) on access to beneficial ownership information for legal entities, trusts and similar arrangements, [according to the Commission's infringement package](https://eaccny.com/news/chapternews/european-commission-eu-takes-action-to-ensure-complete-and-timely-transposition-of-eu-directives-2/). The deadline was 10 July 2025.

## How the EU got here

The Fifth AML Directive had required Member States to make beneficial ownership information on companies available to any member of the public. On 22 November 2022, in Joined Cases C-37/20 and C-601/20 (WM and Sovim SA v Luxembourg Business Registers), the Court of Justice declared that rule invalid. As [eucrim reports](https://eucrim.eu/news/cjeu-no-unrestricted-access-to-data-of-beneficial-owners/), the Court found that general public access seriously interfered with the rights to private life and data protection under Articles 7 and 8 of the EU Charter, and was not limited to what is strictly necessary. eucrim also reported that Luxembourg and the Netherlands were moving to restrict access to their registers after the ruling.

## What AMLD6 changed

[Directive (EU) 2024/1640](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202401640) (AMLD6) rebuilt access around a "legitimate interest" test, in two stages.

**By 10 July 2025:** [Article 74](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/aml/aml6-directive/article-74/) amended Articles 30(5) and 31(4) of the Fourth AML Directive. Authorities and FIUs keep unrestricted access, obliged entities get access as part of customer due diligence, and any person or organisation that can demonstrate a legitimate interest must be able to see at least the beneficial owner's name, month and year of birth, country of residence, nationality, and the nature and extent of the interest held. This is the deadline the 11 states missed.

**By 10 July 2026:** [Article 78](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/aml/aml6-directive/article-78/) sets this date for the full access regime in Articles 11, 12, 13 and 15. Under [Article 12](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/aml/aml6-directive/article-12/), several groups are presumed to have a legitimate interest, including journalists and civil society organisations working on money laundering, people about to enter a transaction with the entity, third-country obliged entities doing due diligence, and public procurement authorities. Most of the rest of AMLD6 applies from 10 July 2027.

## Why it matters for KYB

Registers are one of the first sources a compliance team checks when verifying who owns a business customer. When a country is slow to set up access, the data is still there but harder to reach, and the process differs from one member state to the next. In practice teams fall back on company documents, declarations from the customer and commercial data providers.

[KYB platforms](/providers/kyb/) package registry data from many countries. [FullCircl](/providers/fullcircl/), now sold by nCino as nCino Identity Solutions, offers KYB data on 365 million entities, and [Vespia](/providers/vespia/), now part of Veriff, covers business registry data in more than 300 jurisdictions; Veriff sells it through a KYB limited access program rather than as a standalone product. Register access still matters underneath those tools, because what a vendor can pull from a country depends on what that country releases. ## What has happened since

**A second, larger round.** In late September 2026 the Commission [sent letters of formal notice to 18 member states](https://europeansting.com/2026/09/28/commission-takes-action-to-ensure-complete-and-timely-transposition-of-eu-directives-13/) that had not declared full transposition of AMLD6 Articles 11, 12, 13 and 15 by 10 July 2026: Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, Czechia, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Lithuania, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Poland, Portugal, Romania and Spain. They have two months to respond before the Commission can move to a reasoned opinion. [IFC Review notes](https://www.ifcreview.com/news/2026/october/eu-eu-member-states-told-to-comply-with-6amld-beneficial-ownership-access-rules/) that seven states appear on both the 2025 and 2026 lists: Belgium, Croatia, Cyprus, Estonia, Germany, Greece and Poland.

**The 2025 cases.** We did not find a published reasoned opinion, referral or closure for the 11 states from September 2025 when we checked in October 2026.

**Italy's register.** On 21 May 2026, in Joined Cases C-684/24 and C-685/24, the Court of Justice held that access to beneficial ownership data for people with a legitimate interest is compatible with Articles 7 and 8 of the Charter, but that a beneficial owner refused an exemption must be able to get interim judicial protection before disclosure. According to an [analysis by LCA Studio Legale](https://www.lcalex.it/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Italys-Beneficial-Ownwership-Register.-The-Implications-of-the-CJEU-Judgment-of-21-May-2026.pdf), Italy's register had been suspended since December 2023, and the Council of State still has to apply the ruling.
