The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) [announced on 22 July 2025](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/eng/news-and-media/press-releases/2025/07/20250722-4/) that it had disciplined three banks under section 21 of the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (AMLO, Cap. 615). The common thread was ongoing monitoring: none of the three had effective procedures for continuously monitoring their customer relationships.

## The three penalties

| Bank | Penalty | Other action | Period |
|---|---|---|---|
| Indian Overseas Bank, Hong Kong Branch (IOBHK) | HK$8.5M | Reprimand, look-back review, remedial plan, independent assessment | May 2021 to January 2024 |
| Bank of Communications (Hong Kong) Limited (BCOM(HK)) | HK$4M | None | January 2018 to December 2024 |
| Bank of Communications Co., Ltd., Hong Kong Branch | HK$3.7M | None | January 2018 to December 2024 |

In setting the amounts, the HKMA weighed how serious the findings were, the need for a deterrent message to the industry, any remedial work the banks had done, and the fact that none had a prior AMLO disciplinary record and all cooperated.

## What went wrong at Bank of Communications

The two Bank of Communications entities share a transaction monitoring system. According to the HKMA's [statement on BCOM(HK)](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/media/eng/doc/key-information/press-release/2025/20250722e4a2.pdf), the bank had no effective procedures to make sure that system covered every transaction it was meant to cover. Gaps opened when the core banking system was implemented, when later system changes were made and when new services launched. Certain transaction types were never loaded into the system at all. The HKMA did not say which types.

The result: scrutiny was not enough to confirm that transactions matched what the bank knew about the customer for about 97,000 customers at BCOM(HK) and about 3,900 at the [Hong Kong branch](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/media/eng/doc/key-information/press-release/2025/20250722e4a3.pdf). Both breached sections 5(1)(b) and 19(3) of AMLO Schedule 2. Major omissions were fixed by September 2022 and all of them by December 2024, and the HKMA gave credit for that remediation.

This is a data-coverage failure, not a bad rule or a slow analyst. Any bank that has migrated a core system or launched a product without checking which feeds reach its monitoring engine has the same exposure. ## What went wrong at Indian Overseas Bank

IOBHK's case was broader. The [HKMA statement](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/media/eng/doc/key-information/press-release/2025/20250722e4a1.pdf) lists three breaches of AMLO Schedule 2:

- **Section 23, management oversight.** Senior management did not clearly lead on AML/CFT, key control issues were not properly discussed in committee, and the Compliance Officer lacked enough AML/CFT knowledge. Monitoring alerts were not followed up properly as a result.
- **Section 19(3), procedures.** No policies set out which department did what in transaction monitoring, and staff training was inadequate.
- **Section 5(1)(c), unusual transactions.** For ten customers, the branch did not examine the background and purpose of complex, unusually large or unusually patterned transactions, and did not record findings in writing.

Beyond the fine and reprimand, IOBHK must review past alerts and report results to the HKMA, submit a remedial plan, and after the plan is in place, provide a report from an independent external adviser on whether the fix works.

Raymond Chan, the HKMA's Executive Director (Enforcement and AML), said: "Effective transaction monitoring enables timely identification and reporting of suspicious transactions."

## Practical checks for compliance teams

1. Map every product, channel and payment type to the feed that carries it into monitoring, and test it after each system change.
2. Put a sign-off step into product launches that confirms monitoring coverage before go-live.
3. Track alert follow-up times and escalate backlogs to senior management.
4. Record the reasoning when an unusual transaction is cleared.

## What has happened since

We checked the HKMA's [enforcement press release page](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/eng/news-and-media/press-releases/enforcement/) in October 2026. The entries it shows after 22 July 2025 are joint HKMA and SFC actions (EFG Bank AG on 11 December 2025 and HSBC on 26 August 2025) that concern securities rules, not the AMLO. We found no further AMLO disciplinary action against a bank in that period. The HKMA has not published the outcome of IOBHK's look-back review or the independent assessment, so we cannot say whether the remediation has been signed off.
