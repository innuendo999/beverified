The EU set itself a hard date for digital identity, and that date is now six weeks away. Every member state is supposed to offer its residents a European Digital Identity Wallet before the end of 2026. A year later the obligation reaches the private sector: banks, payment firms and other businesses that have to authenticate users strongly must accept the wallet when a customer chooses to use it.

If you buy identity verification for EU onboarding, the useful question now is which countries will have a working wallet, and when. So far the answer is uneven, and the Commission says so in its own report.

## What the regulation requires, and when

[Regulation (EU) 2024/1183](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ%3AL_202401183), the revision of eIDAS usually called [eIDAS 2](/glossary/eidas-2/), sets two clocks. Both run from the entry into force of the implementing acts referred to in Article 5a(23) and Article 5c(6).

- **Member states, 24 months.** Under Article 5a(1), "each Member State shall provide at least one European Digital Identity Wallet" within 24 months of those acts.
- **Regulated private firms, 36 months.** Article 5f(2) covers private relying parties that must use strong user authentication for online identification, whether under Union or national law or under a contract. The sectors it lists include banking, financial services, transport, energy, health, telecommunications and digital infrastructure. They must accept the wallet no later than 36 months after the same acts, and only at the user's voluntary request. Micro and small enterprises are exempt.
- **Very large online platforms.** Article 5f(3) adds platforms designated under the Digital Services Act: where they require user authentication, they must accept the wallet at the user's request, for "the minimum data necessary" for the service.

The first package of implementing acts was adopted on 28 November 2024 and published on 4 December 2024. [Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2979](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2024/2979/oj/eng/pdf), on the wallet's integrity and core functionalities, enters into force on the twentieth day after publication, which EUR-Lex records as 24 December 2024. Counting from that date gives 24 December 2026 for wallets and 24 December 2027 for private acceptance. That is our calculation. The Commission itself speaks of "the end of 2026" and its [wallet factpage](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/european-digital-identity-wallet) says wallets "will be available as from 2026".

Two rules matter for onboarding design. Use of the wallet "shall be voluntary" (Article 5a(15)), so nobody can be made to use one. And a firm that wants to rely on wallets has to register as a relying party in the member state where it is established (Article 5b(1)).

## The Commission's own doubts

The Commission ran its first large interoperability test, the EUDI Wallets Launchpad, in Brussels on 10 to 12 December 2025. Its [summary page](https://ec.europa.eu/digital-building-blocks/sites/spaces/EUDIGITALIDENTITYWALLET/pages/964167040/Discover+the+key+takeaways+from+Launchpad+2025) is upbeat: "The ecosystem is ready. Now it's time to deliver." It reports 16 countries testing, 11 of them member states, with 74% of 570 wallet-to-wallet tests passing and 44% of 101 tests against the reference implementation.

The [full report](https://ec.europa.eu/digital-building-blocks/sites/download/attachments/964167040/EUDI%20Wallets%20Launchpad%20-%20Event%20Key%20Takeways%20report.pdf) is more careful. It says "fewer than one quarter of Member States participated with EUDI Wallet enabled applications" and that "only a limited number of Member States are likely to meet the November 2026 EUDIW deadline". It also calls trust infrastructure onboarding "a weak point" and notes that the trust lists made available for testing were not used. (The report says November; the regulation's arithmetic points to late December.)

## What this means for KYC onboarding

The wallet does not remove the duty to [know your customer](/glossary/know-your-customer/). What changes is the evidence. A customer with a wallet can share verified person identification data, and only the attributes the firm asks for, instead of photographing an ID card and taking a selfie. That is the [reusable digital identity](/glossary/reusable-digital-identity/) model with a government issuer behind it. AMLA's draft standards point the same way: as we covered in our note on the [AMLA customer due diligence standards](/news/amla-sends-cdd-standards-to-the-commission/), they say electronic identification at eIDAS level substantial or high, including wallets, should be used wherever possible.

For onboarding teams, the dates translate into three things:

- **Plan for two routes for years.** Wallets are voluntary and arrive country by country. A flow built for 2027 still needs document, NFC and liveness checks for everyone without a wallet, and a clean hand-off between the two.
- **Registration is your job.** Relying party registration happens in your home member state. Ask your vendor what it handles and what stays with you.
- **The 2027 date is closer than it looks.** For banks and other firms covered by Article 5f(2), acceptance is a legal obligation from late 2027, and integration, legal review and testing against several national wallets all take time.

## Questions to ask identity verification vendors

We have not seen a public source showing that any provider we review accepts EUDI Wallet presentations in production, so we make no such claim here. Ask instead, and ask for evidence:

- Which national wallets can your flow accept today, in test and in production, and which are on the roadmap with dates?
- Do you act as the relying party, or do we register and you process on our behalf?
- How do you validate a presentation (issuer trust lists, revocation, device binding), and what do we keep as evidence for our CDD file?
- If a customer has no wallet, or the presentation fails, does the flow fall back to document and biometric checks without starting again?
- Will you accept attestations of attributes, such as address or age, alongside identity data?
