Alabama has a law that makes app stores check ages, and it is now asking the public how those checks should work. Attorney General Steve Marshall [announced on 19 November 2026](https://www.alabamaag.gov/attorney-general-marshall-seeks-public-input-on-future-age-verification-rules-for-app-stores/) a Request for Information "seeking comments, evidence, data, and other information" for the rules his office has to write under the Alabama App Store Accountability Act, Act 2026-59. "Reasonable and effective age-verification rules start with good information," Marshall said in the release.

## What the Act asks of app stores

The [Request for Information](https://www.alabamaag.gov/alabama-app-store-accountability-act/) cites the law as Ala. Acts No. 2026-59, Ala. Code §8-19K-1 and following. It covers an "app store provider", meaning an entity that "owns, operates, or controls an app store that allows account holders in the state to download apps onto a mobile device."

According to the Attorney General's release, the Act prohibits app store providers and developers from letting minors access apps without parental consent. App stores must request each account holder's age category and then verify it. They can do that in two ways: through commercially available methods, or through a system that complies with rules the Attorney General adopts. Section 10 of the Act tells the Attorney General to adopt rules "establishing processes and means" for app stores to verify whether an account holder is a minor. This request is the first step toward those rules.

The law puts the age check at the store, before a single app is opened. That is a different model from the one we covered in [Ofcom's age checks at 16](/news/ofcom-age-checks-at-16/), where each regulated service runs its own [age assurance](/glossary/age-assurance/) and app stores mostly rely on self-declared ages.

## The 26 questions

The request is organised into groups, and respondents do not have to answer all of them. The groups and what they ask:

| Group | What the Attorney General wants to know |
|---|---|
| Considerations | Which factors matter when judging an age verification method, which properties are desirable, and what makes viability hard to assess |
| Commercially available option | Whether it is workable, what gaps the rules need to fill, and what makes a method "reasonably designed to ensure accuracy" |
| Second option | How a rules-based system should complement the commercial option, and which entities the commercial option would not work for |
| Methods in general | Every existing method, with its accuracy, how minors get around it, privacy, data collected and kept, accessibility for disabled users, legal challenges, cost and fit for smaller providers; plus methods likely to arrive soon |
| App store practice | Methods app stores use today, methods they are considering, and methods they looked at and rejected |
| Data retention | Best practice for keeping age verification data, and the minimum data needed to audit compliance |
| Other laws | Similar rules in other jurisdictions, experience complying with them, and how the Act interacts with COPPA |
| Systemic questions | Allowing several compliance methods, methods that only work in combination, a catch-all for future methods, and stopping circumvention by minors without unduly burdening adults |

The release names the audience: "Alabama parents and children, consumer advocacy groups, privacy advocacy groups, industry participants, and other members of the public." Responses go through an [online form](https://www.alabamaag.gov/alabama-app-store-accountability-act-response/) that takes a name, title, company, email, phone number and up to three PDF files of 10 MB each. The deadline is 8 January 2027.

## The timing problem

[WSFA reports](https://www.fox10tv.com/2026/11/19/alabama-ag-asks-input-app-store-age-verification-rules-amid-lawsuit/) that the Act is scheduled to take effect on 1 January 2027. The comment period ends a week after that, so the Attorney General's rules cannot be in place on day one. Neither the release nor the request gives a date for draft or final rules.

## The CCIA lawsuit

The Computer & Communications Industry Association, a tech trade group, [sued on 28 October 2026](https://ccianet.org/news/2026/10/ccia-files-lawsuit-arguing-alabamas-app-store-law-likely-violates-first-amendment/), alleging that the law violates First Amendment protections. The [complaint](https://ccianet.org/wp-content/uploads/securepdfs/2026/10/2026-10-28-LITIG-CCIA-v.-Marshall-Alabama-ASAA.pdf), filed in the US District Court for the Middle District of Alabama against Marshall in his official capacity, asks the court to "declare the Act unconstitutional and enjoin its enforcement as to CCIA's app store and developer members." These are CCIA's claims; the court has not ruled on them.

According to [WSFA](https://www.fox10tv.com/2026/11/19/alabama-ag-asks-input-app-store-age-verification-rules-amid-lawsuit/), CCIA said on Tuesday, 17 November, that it had reached an agreement with the Attorney General's office to delay further legal proceedings until Alabama adopts rules on acceptable age verification methods. We have not seen the agreement itself or a court order, so we report it as WSFA describes it. None of the three Attorney General pages mentions the lawsuit.

## What it means for age verification buyers and vendors

The questions read like a vendor due diligence list. Accuracy, circumvention by minors, data kept after the check, cost for smaller providers and accessibility are what a buyer should already be asking any [age verification](/glossary/age-verification/) provider. The answers Alabama collects could set the bar for what counts as "reasonably designed to ensure accuracy" in at least one US state.

Two of the groups are worth preparing for now:

- Data retention has its own questions, including the minimum data needed to audit compliance. Vendors should be ready to say what they keep after a check and for how long.
- Circumvention is asked about twice, once per method and once at system level. Methods such as [facial age estimation](/glossary/facial-age-estimation/) will likely need evidence on spoofing and on error near the age line, not only a general accuracy figure. ## What happens next

Responses are due by 8 January 2027. The Act is scheduled to take effect on 1 January 2027, and the Attorney General's rules will follow the comment period. The CCIA case stays on hold under the agreement WSFA reported. We will update this story when draft rules are published or the court acts.
