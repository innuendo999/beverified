Corlytics, which tracks regulatory penalties worldwide, counted 25 fines of $1 million or more between July and September 2026, worth roughly $2 billion in total, [according to FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/11/06/data-regulators-land-1-4bn-blow-as-q3-fines-reach-2bn/). Two data protection fines, against Uber and Google, made up 71% of that value. AML still accounted for many of the cases. Corlytics' [Q3 report](https://www.corlytics.com/enforcement-report-q3-2026/) lists five of the quarter's largest fines as AML cases, the biggest being FinCEN's $125 million penalty on UBS Financial Services. In October Bank of America followed, with penalties from three Treasury and banking regulators on one day.

## Bank of America: $1.2 billion from FinCEN, $750 million from the OCC

On 13 October 2026 [FinCEN assessed a $1.2 billion penalty](https://www.fincen.gov/news/news-releases/fincen-assesses-12-billion-penalty-against-bank-america) on Bank of America, N.A. On the same day the [OCC imposed $750 million](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2026/nr-occ-2026-89.html), which it calls the largest civil money penalty in its history. The two are not added together. FinCEN's [consent order](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2026-10/BoA-Consent-Order-Execution-Final.pdf) credits the $750 million paid to the OCC and leaves $450 million payable to Treasury.

FinCEN found three failures: an AML program that did not meet the Bank Secrecy Act's minimum requirements, missing due diligence on correspondent accounts for foreign financial institutions, and suspicious activity reports that were not filed or were filed late. Bank of America admitted the violations were willful. The consent order covers 1 January 2018 to 1 July 2025 and says the bank missed or delayed reporting of over $5 billion of suspicious activity, about $1 billion of it tied to national security and other priority risks.

| Action | Regulator | Amount | Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Civil money penalty, BSA/AML | FinCEN | $1.2 billion (credits the OCC's $750 million) | 13 October 2026 |
| Civil money penalty, BSA/AML program | OCC | $750 million | 13 October 2026 |
| Settlement, 475 apparent sanctions violations | OFAC | $11,662,930 | 13 October 2026 |
| Civil money penalty, BSA (recidivist) | FinCEN on UBS Financial Services | $125 million | 3 August 2026 |

## The monitoring system set the bar too high

The consent order describes the bank's main monitoring system in detail. Alerts were grouped, and a group needed 20 points within a 13-month window before anyone investigated it. Some risk factors were weighted far below what their results justified: FinCEN cites large cash deposits at ATMs, whose alerts turned into SARs more than half the time but carried 1 point. The threshold was cut to 10 or 12 points and the window to four months only in December 2023. A lookback in 2024 found nearly 8,900 SARs that should have been filed, covering almost $4 billion.

Coverage had gaps as well. Deposits of negotiable instruments were largely unmonitored, and credit card coverage was not enough. For several years more than a third of investigations started from subpoenas and referrals, not from the system's own alerts. The OCC adds a systemic gap that let potential trade-based money laundering through, a BSA audit function that missed long-running problems, and staff who were not trained to spot and report suspicious activity.

## Correspondent accounts got "simplified" due diligence

The second failure is correspondent banking. Until late 2024, the bank's policy allowed simplified due diligence for correspondent accounts it kept for its own foreign branches and affiliates. FinCEN looked at 12 of them. Their files carried generic business descriptions such as "treasury services", said little about the supervisory regime beyond the regulator's name, and relied on risk assessments that were several years old. Those branches held accounts for more than 250 non-bank financial institutions, including money services businesses, and their payments did not look like bank-to-bank settlement flows.

## An order already in place

The OCC had put Bank of America under a cease and desist order in December 2024 to fix its BSA/AML program. According to the OCC, more than a year later the bank still was not escalating potentially suspicious activity to investigators and had not completed many of the steps the order required. 
FinCEN's order now requires four independent reviews paid for by the bank: a SAR lookback from January 2022 with a final report no later than December 2028, a review of suspicious activity monitoring with an end-state report no later than July 2028, a customer and foreign correspondent due diligence review, and a data governance review.

## OFAC: alerts that matched and were not worked

[OFAC settled](https://ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions/20261013_33) 475 apparent violations with Bank of America for $11,662,930, across the Russia, Belarus, Iran, Burma, Somalia, narcotics and global terrorism programs. OFAC says the bank processed payments involving blocked banks, did not prioritise alerts where a customer's name and date of birth exactly matched a blocked person, was slow to update its watchlists after new designations, and mishandled two business accounts linked to blocked persons through human error. OFAC treated the case as non-egregious, and most of the violations were not voluntarily self-disclosed. [Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0657) put the three actions together at more than $1.21 billion.

## UBS Financial Services: the same gap, years after a promised fix

On 3 August 2026 [FinCEN assessed $125 million](https://www.fincen.gov/news/news-releases/fincen-assesses-historic-125-million-penalty-against-ubs-financial-services-inc) on UBS Financial Services Inc. (UBSFS), which it describes as the largest BSA penalty ever imposed on a broker-dealer. It is FinCEN's second action against the firm. A December 2018 consent order had imposed $14.5 million, in part for not monitoring foreign currency wires.

FinCEN says the same weakness in an automated monitoring system then left over 50,000 foreign currency wires, worth more than $10 billion, unmonitored after UBSFS had promised to fix it. FinCEN also found that the firm did not assess source of wealth and money laundering risk for some high-risk customers with ties to Russia and Latin America, ignored negative news alleging corruption, fraud and money laundering, and reported hundreds of suspicious transactions late. UBSFS admitted willful violations. It must commission a third-party lookback and an independent review of its AML program, and FinCEN will waive up to $15 million of the expenses once that work is completed to its satisfaction.

## FCA: six Annex 1 lenders restricted

The UK action was not a fine. On 23 October 2026 the [FCA said](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-restricts-6-firms-following-concerns-over-anti-money-laundering-controls) it had placed restrictions on six unregulated lenders registered as Annex 1 firms after raising concerns about their AML policies, controls and procedures while assessing their applications. Annex 1 firms are registered for AML supervision under the Money Laundering Regulations but are not authorised by the FCA for anything else. Each restriction was imposed at the firm's own request.

| Firm | Restrictions from | What they cover |
|---|---|---|
| Kingscrown Finance Ltd | 21 April 2026, more on 25 September 2026 | Limits on taking payments from existing customers, orderly wind-down of the loan book |
| MOMSB Ltd and Momentus Capital Ltd | 7 October 2026 | No new customers or new lending with limited exceptions, limits on payments, independent review of the AML framework |
| Grosvenor Funding Ltd and Grosvenor Funding (North West) Ltd | 9 October 2026 | Same types of measure as MOMSB and Momentus |
| Abbital Ltd | 15 October 2026 | Conditions on new customers, new lending and payments, independent review of AML systems and controls |

The FCA is also working through responses from about 900 registered Annex 1 firms. It reminded regulated firms that a lender's registration under the Money Laundering Regulations does not replace their own due diligence on it. The FCA has not made findings of money laundering against the six firms. For background on the UK rules, see our [glossary entry on the Money Laundering Regulations](/glossary/uk-money-laundering-regulations/).

## What buyers of AML screening and monitoring software should check

None of these orders blames a software vendor, and none names one. The failures are about how the institutions configured, fed and governed their systems. A buyer can still test for the same weak points before signing or during a proof of concept:

- **Coverage by flow.** Ask for a report of which transaction types, channels and currencies reach the monitoring engine, and how you would see a new product or currency that does not. UBSFS's gap was one currency type; Bank of America's included negotiable instrument deposits. The HKMA [fined three Hong Kong banks](/news/hkma-fines-three-hong-kong-banks-hk16-2m-for-aml-monitoring-gaps/) in 2025 for the same kind of gap.
- **Tuning you can explain.** Check that scenario weights, scoring thresholds and look-back windows are visible and versioned, and that the product shows conversion from alert to SAR by scenario. A factor with a 50% SAR yield and a weight of 1 point is the kind of mismatch that report exposes.
- **Watchlist latency.** Ask how fast a new OFAC designation reaches screening, and whether exact name and date of birth matches are prioritised over fuzzy ones. Our [OFAC screening guide](/publications/how-to-conduct-ofac-screening-step-by-step-guide/) covers the list mechanics.
- **Correspondent and affiliate due diligence.** If you hold accounts for foreign banks or your own foreign affiliates, the CDD tool should record purpose, expected activity, supervisory regime and a dated risk assessment for each, and flag files that go stale.
- **Negative news in the risk score.** Adverse media should feed customer risk ratings, not sit in a separate queue. See our piece on [negative news screening](/news/why-negative-news-screening-is-a-must-have-for-aml-compliance/).
- **Evidence for remediation.** Audit trails, change logs and reports an independent consultant can rerun matter once a regulator asks for a lookback.

Our [transaction monitoring guide](/guide/transaction-monitoring-a-complete-guide/) goes deeper on coverage testing and tuning. To compare tools, see the [AML software ranking](/providers/aml/) and the [AML ranking for banks](/providers/aml/banks/); scores follow our [methodology](/methodology/).

## What happens next

Bank of America's independent reviews run to 2028, with the SAR lookback's final report due no later than December 2028. UBSFS has its lookback and AML program review to complete. In the UK, the FCA is still assessing the Annex 1 information request and has said it will act where firms cannot show they manage their money laundering risk.
