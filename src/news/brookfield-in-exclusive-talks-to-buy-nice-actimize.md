NICE, the Nasdaq-listed software company, has spent most of the past year looking for a buyer for Actimize, its anti-money laundering, fraud and compliance business. The latest report puts Brookfield, the Canadian investment group, in exclusive talks to buy it. For the banks and other firms that run Actimize, a change of owner matters. Here is what has been reported, what has not been confirmed, and what customers can ask while the process runs.

## What was reported in September

On 23 September 2026, [Sky News reported](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/brookfield-talks-buy-financial-crime-094500041.html) that Brookfield was in exclusive talks to buy Actimize from NICE for about $2 billion. Its sources said a deal had not been finalised and could still fall apart. Brookfield declined to comment, and the report carried no statement from NICE.

The buyer would be Brookfield's financial infrastructure business, part of its private equity arm. Sky News says it was set up about three years ago with help from Sir Ron Kalifa, the former head of Worldpay, and that in 2025 it bought a stake in Barclays' merchant acquiring business. [CTech, Calcalist's English site](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/iazqvedg2), also reported that Brookfield had secured exclusivity.

## How the sale got here

| When | What was reported |
|---|---|
| 2007 | NICE buys Actimize for $280 million. It becomes a NICE division in 2009. |
| November 2025 | Calcalist reports that NICE has put Actimize up for sale at $1.5 to 2 billion, with Goldman Sachs and J.P. Morgan advising. |
| May 2026 | Five bidders reach the second round with non-binding offers around $2.5 billion: Advent International, Veritas Capital, New Mountain Capital, Stone Point Capital and SymphonyAI. |
| May 2026 | NICE's CEO, Scott Russell, tells investors the company has worked with advisers on its non-CX assets, naming financial crime and compliance and public safety. He says no decisions have been made. |
| 23 September 2026 | Brookfield is reported to be in exclusive talks at about $2 billion. |

The [November 2025 report](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/syc7hf11zwx) set the asking price. The [May 2026 report](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/bjt5joeawe) said final binding offers could differ from the first round. [A-Team Insight](https://a-teaminsight.com/blog/nice-earnings-call-puts-reported-actimize-sale-update-in-context/) covered what Russell said on the first-quarter earnings call. None of the bidder names or prices has been confirmed by NICE.

## Why NICE is selling

According to Calcalist, NICE wants to concentrate on cloud and AI software for customer service. The sale would also help pay for that shift, including its $955 million purchase of Cognigy, a German conversational AI company. NICE had $667 million in cash at the end of the third quarter of 2025.

Actimize is still a large business inside NICE. Calcalist puts its 2024 revenue at $453.5 million, 16.6% of NICE's total, and its operating profit at $158.3 million, about 29% of the group's. In 2019 it brought in more than half of NICE's operating profit.

## What Actimize sells

Actimize is one of the broadest financial crime suites we review. Its products include suspicious activity monitoring, WL-X sanctions and watchlist screening, X-Sight Entity Risk, customer due diligence workflows and regulatory reporting. AML Essentials and Xceed are aimed at mid-sized institutions, credit unions and community banks. ## What customers and buyers can ask now

Nothing changes for customers until a deal is signed and closed. A change of owner can still affect contracts, support and the product roadmap, so these are fair questions for a renewal or a new purchase:

- What does the contract say about assignment or a change of control, and can you exit or renegotiate if the owner changes?
- Which products are on a committed roadmap for the next two years, and which are in maintenance?
- Will support teams, hosting arrangements and data processing terms stay the same under a new owner?
- Is the price fixed for the full term, or can it be revised at renewal?
