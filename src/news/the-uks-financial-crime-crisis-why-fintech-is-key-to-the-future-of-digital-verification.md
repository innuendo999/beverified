The Home Office calls fraud the largest reported crime type in England and Wales. Plenty of it runs through companies that are not what they claim to be: shell firms opening accounts, fake suppliers sending invoices, impersonated businesses taking payments. In March 2025 CFIT, the Centre for Finance, Innovation and Technology founded with the City of London Corporation, put forward a fix aimed at that gap.

## The Digital Company ID plan

CFIT [published its plan](https://cfit.org.uk/cfit-publishes-digital-company-id-plan-to-slash-fraud-saving-uk-businesses-billions-annually.) on 6 March 2025. It came from a group of about 70 experts drawn from financial services, academia, technology, government agencies and regulators. The idea is a reusable digital identity for a UK business: verified once, then shared securely with banks, lenders, suppliers and public bodies, instead of every counterparty repeating the same checks.

CFIT said the scheme could help offset the UK's £6.8 billion annual cost of fraud and cut financial institutions' compliance costs by £1.7 billion a year. It made seven recommendations, including:

- build a prototype, ideally with support from the FCA's innovation services;
- enable reciprocal, secure sharing of economic crime data;
- appoint a lead authority for implementation and governance;
- push adoption through government use, such as procurement, tax filings and Companies House confirmation statements.

Lloyds Bank, NatWest Group, Monzo Business and Sage were among the organisations quoted in support.

## Why business identity is the weak point

Onboarding a company is slower and more manual than onboarding a person. A bank has to confirm the entity exists, map its owners and controllers, then verify the individuals behind it. Our original story cited research on corporate digital identity (CDI) from Encompass and Chartis. Encompass [modelled](https://encompasscorporation.com/blog/future-proofing-client-onboarding-with-cdi-a-roadmap-to-long-term-sustainable-value) a 21% cut in total onboarding cost in the first year from efficiency alone, 57% once commercial benefits like faster onboarding and lower churn are counted, and efficiency savings of up to 59% within five years. These are vendor models, not measured results.

This is the work [KYB software](/providers/kyb/) automates today. Examples with UK roots include [FullCircl](/providers/fullcircl/), now owned by nCino and sold as nCino Identity Solutions, which combines registry data with KYB, KYC and AML checks, and [SmartSearch](/providers/smartsearch/), which verifies individuals and businesses for firms regulated under JMLSG guidance. ## The fraud numbers, updated

Our March 2025 article quoted older figures. Here are the latest ones published by UK Finance, the ONS and the Home Office:

| Measure | Latest figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Payment fraud losses, 2025 | £1.28 billion, up 4% | UK Finance |
| Unauthorised fraud losses, 2025 | £703.4 million, down 5% | UK Finance |
| Authorised push payment losses, 2025 | £576.4 million, up 19% | UK Finance |
| Fraud cases, 2025 | nearly 4.1 million, up 11% | UK Finance |
| Unauthorised fraud prevented, 2025 | £1.68 billion | UK Finance |
| Fraud incidents, year ending March 2026 | 4.5 million | ONS |
| Cost of fraud, England and Wales, 2023 to 2024 | at least £14.4 billion | Home Office |

UK Finance's [Annual Fraud Report 2026](https://www.ukfinance.org.uk/policy-and-guidance/reports/annual-fraud-report-2026) says the £1.28 billion loss figure is the highest since the pandemic peak in 2021. The [full report](https://www.ukfinance.org.uk/system/files/2026-06/UK%20Finance%20Fraud%20Report%202026.pdf) splits the total: losses where the victim is tricked into sending money (authorised push payment fraud) rose, while unauthorised losses fell.

The [ONS Crime Survey](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/crimeandjustice/bulletins/crimeinenglandandwales/yearendingmarch2026), published on 23 July 2026, estimates 4.5 million fraud incidents out of 9.6 million headline crime incidents. That is not a statistically significant change from the 4.2 million of the year before. Police-recorded fraud is missing from this release because Action Fraud was replaced by the new Report Fraud service.

## What has happened since

The government has picked up the idea. The Home Office [Fraud Strategy 2026 to 2029](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fraud-strategy-2026-to-2029/fraud-strategy-2026-to-2029-disrupting-crime-supporting-economic-resilience-and-delivering-justice-accessible) says it will consider digital company identities, building on the CFIT blueprint, and promotes certified Digital Verification Services under the UK digital identity trust framework. It also commits more than £250 million between 2026 and 2029 and says fraud made up 45% of all crime in the Crime Survey for the year ending September 2025.

On 17 March 2026 CFIT's Digital Company ID Coalition [presented its results](https://cfit.org.uk/?p=3467): a pathway to a live, interoperable prototype, priority use cases (financial services onboarding, supplier checks, marketplaces and government reporting) and a governance framework. Coordination now moves to a market-led model run by the City of London Corporation, with CFIT advising.

Companies House has started checking identities too. Since [18 November 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/companies-house-confirms-identity-verification-rollout-from-18-november-2025), new directors and people with significant control must verify through GOV.UK One Login or an Authorised Corporate Service Provider, and existing directors confirm verification with their next confirmation statement.
