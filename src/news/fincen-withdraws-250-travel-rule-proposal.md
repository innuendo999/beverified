Two Travel Rule decisions landed a week apart and point in different directions. In the United States, regulators dropped a six-year-old plan to make the rule bite on much smaller transfers. At the global level, the FATF said it will look hard at countries where crypto firms are big but supervision is thin. Neither one changes what a firm has to do today, but both matter for anyone choosing [Travel Rule software](/providers/travel-rule/).

## What FinCEN withdrew

On 6 November 2026 [FinCEN announced](https://www.fincen.gov/news/news-releases/fincen-announces-withdrawal-proposed-rule-related-recordkeeping-and-travel-rule) that it is withdrawing, jointly with the Federal Reserve Board, a notice of proposed rulemaking on the Recordkeeping Rule and the Travel Rule for convertible virtual currencies. The [withdrawal notice](https://www.fincen.gov/resources/statutes-regulations/federal-register-notices/withdrawal-proposed-rule-related) (FR Doc. 2026-22809) applies from its publication in the Federal Register on 9 November 2026, according to [RegReporting Desk](https://regreportingdesk.com/fincen-travel-rule-threshold-proposal-withdrawn/), which read the public inspection copy.

The proposal dates from 27 October 2020 (85 FR 68005). It would have done two things:

| Element | 2020 proposal | After the withdrawal |
|---|---|---|
| Threshold for transfers that begin or end outside the US | Cut from $3,000 to $250 | Stays at $3,000 |
| Threshold for domestic transfers | $3,000, unchanged | $3,000 |
| Definitions of "payment order" and "transmittal order" | Rewritten to cover convertible virtual currency and digital assets with legal tender status | Not rewritten |

The agencies drew close to 3,000 comments, most of them form letters. The notice says the agencies will take no further action on the proposal, and that withdrawing it gives the public certainty that no final rule will be issued without another chance to comment, as [Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/fincen-fed-withdraw-proposal-that-would-have-written-crypto-into-travel-rule-definitions/) reports. RegReporting Desk adds that the notice points to the time that has passed since 2020 and to the GENIUS Act, the 2025 stablecoin law, as the framework for judging any future change for virtual currency.

This is the third crypto-related FinCEN proposal withdrawn since early October. The unhosted wallet proposal and the proposed special measure on virtual currency mixing were withdrawn on 6 October 2026.

## What has not changed in the US

The [Travel Rule](/glossary/travel-rule/) still applies. Covered banks and non-bank financial institutions keep the same duties at the same $3,000 line. FinCEN's 2019 guidance on virtual currency business models (FIN-2019-G001), which treats virtual currency transmittals as covered at $3,000 or its equivalent, is not mentioned in the notice and was still listed without changes on 7 November 2026, RegReporting Desk found.

So nothing needs to be switched off. A firm that already sends originator and beneficiary data below $3,000, for example because it serves customers in places with lower or no thresholds, can keep doing so. The withdrawal only removes a stricter US rule that had been pending since 2020.

## What the FATF decided on 30 October

The [FATF plenary in Paris](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfgeneral/outcomes-fatf-plenary-october-2026.html), the first under the UK presidency of Giles Thomson, ran from 28 to 30 October 2026. For crypto, the main outcome is a [targeted review](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfrecommendations/launch-targeted-review-strengthen-standards-virtual-assets.html) of jurisdictions with materially important virtual asset activity, picked on objective criteria and on their assessment status, with accelerated follow-up for those that fall short. First results are due in June 2027 and more in October 2027. The [US Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0687/) says the review will set out decision points for the next plenary on holding jurisdictions with systemic weaknesses to account.

The review follows the FATF's [July 2026 targeted update](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/news/targeted-updated-va-vasps-2026.html). It found that 83% of surveyed jurisdictions (91 of 109) have passed Travel Rule legislation, up from 73% in 2025, with 11 more working on it. Many of those with a law on the books have not yet supervised or enforced it, which is the gap the new review is aimed at.

Other outcomes that feed straight into country risk settings:

- Bulgaria, Côte d'Ivoire, the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Monaco left the [grey list](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/High-risk-and-other-monitored-jurisdictions/increased-monitoring-october-2026.html). No jurisdiction was added, so 18 remain.
- The FATF warned Vietnam about the pace of its progress and expects tangible results by February 2027.
- The black list is unchanged. Countermeasures against Iran stay in place and cover virtual asset service providers and virtual asset transactions.
- The plenary adopted the mutual evaluations of the United States and Mexico. Both reports will be published in December 2026.
- It also approved a report on the money flows behind scam compounds.

## What this means if you are choosing a Travel Rule provider

For a US-only business nothing has moved: same obligation, same threshold. For a business with customers or counterparties abroad, the FATF review is the bigger signal. Supervisors in the jurisdictions it covers have a reason to start checking Travel Rule compliance before mid-2027.

## What happens next

In the US, any new attempt to lower the threshold would start as a fresh proposal with its own comment period. At the FATF, the next deadline is February 2027 for Vietnam, and the targeted review's first public results follow in June 2027.
