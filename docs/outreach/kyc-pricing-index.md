## LinkedIn summary (about 150 words)

How much does KYC cost? We checked every vendor pricing page we track.

Of the 55 KYC, AML and KYB providers BeVerified reviews, 18 publish at least one price on their own site. For 8 more, the only figures come from marketplaces, public procurement listings or buyer-data services. The other 27 quote on request.

A few things stood out:

- The unit price is rarely the biggest cost for a small team. Monthly minimums and plan fees are.
- AML screening, manual review and ongoing monitoring are often add-ons on top of the base identity check.
- What counts as a billable check (successful only, per match, or every attempt) changes the invoice as much as the rate.

The KYC Pricing Index lists every published figure with its source and the month we checked it, and keeps third-party estimates in a separate table.

## Pitch for compliance media (one paragraph)

BeVerified, an independent review site for KYC, AML and identity verification providers, has published the KYC Pricing Index, a dated table of every price that the 55 providers it reviews publish on their own websites (18 of them do), with marketplace and procurement figures for the vendors that only quote on request kept in a separate, labelled table. Each figure links to the vendor page it came from and carries the month it was checked; the official prices were all re-checked on 2 November 2026, and the tables are generated from the same records as BeVerified's reviews, so they are updated when a vendor changes its price. It could support a piece on how compliance teams budget for onboarding, why monthly minimums matter more than per-check rates for small firms, or how opaque enterprise KYC pricing still is. The full index and methodology are at https://beverified.org/publications/kyc-pricing-index/, and Andrii Klymenko, the editor, is available to discuss the data.
